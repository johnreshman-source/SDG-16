Sustainable Development Goal 16

Awareness for peaceful, fair, and inclusive communities

Small everyday choices—respecting others, following fair processes, and speaking up responsibly—help strengthen trust and institutions.

Peace and non-violence

Peace means resolving disagreements without threats or violence. For example, neighbours can seek a mediated discussion about noise instead of escalating a conflict.

Justice

Justice means decisions should follow fair rules and give people a meaningful chance to be heard. A fair complaint process explains what happens next and treats people consistently.

Equality before the law

Equality before the law means legal protections and responsibilities apply fairly, without arbitrary preference. Public services should use clear, consistent criteria for everyone.

Fundamental human rights

Human rights protect dignity and important freedoms. Respecting a person's safety, privacy, and freedom from discrimination helps everyone participate in community life.

Transparency

Transparency means making decisions and processes understandable and appropriately open. Publishing eligibility rules for a public benefit is one practical example.

Accountability

Accountability means people and institutions can explain decisions and address mistakes. A service that acknowledges a complaint and gives a reasoned response demonstrates accountability.

Access to justice

People need fair ways to understand their rights, obtain legal help, and resolve disputes. Accessible information and legal aid can reduce barriers to seeking help.

Citizen participation

Citizens can contribute through informed discussion, lawful civic engagement, and responsible feedback. Including different voices can help public decisions better reflect community needs.
