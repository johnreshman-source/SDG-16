Rights Awareness

Fundamental Rights in India

Part III of the Constitution of India (Articles 12–35) sets out Fundamental Rights. Their scope, limits, and application depend on constitutional text and case law.

Right to equality

Articles 14–18 address equality before the law, equal protection of the laws, and related protections. Article 14 applies to every person; some other protections are framed specifically for citizens.

Right to freedom

Article 19 lists specified freedoms for citizens, including speech and expression, peaceful assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession, subject to constitutionally permitted restrictions. Articles 20–22 provide other protections, including safeguards in criminal matters and protections concerning life and personal liberty.

Right against exploitation

Articles 23–24 prohibit trafficking and forced labour and restrict the employment of children in hazardous work as set out in the Constitution.

Freedom of religion

Articles 25–28 provide for freedom of conscience and religion, subject to constitutional conditions such as public order, morality, and health.

Cultural and educational rights

Articles 29–30 protect specified cultural and educational interests, including protections for minorities to conserve language, script, and culture and to establish and administer educational institutions.

Constitutional remedies

Article 32 gives people the right to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. High Courts also have writ jurisdiction under Article 226. The appropriate procedure depends on the matter.

Access to justice: Article 39A is a Directive Principle calling on the State to promote equal justice and provide free legal aid in appropriate cases. Eligibility and services are administered under applicable law through legal services authorities; see the official NALSA resource.
